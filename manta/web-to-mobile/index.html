From Tab to Pocket

A deep dive into the platform shift underneath The Year Manta Became a Companion. For years, Manta was a website. But our user doesn't live on a laptop — she lives on her phone, often as her only device. Getting her from a browser tab into a native app sounds like an engineering project. It was mostly a design problem, and an unusually delicate one given who we were asking to make the leap.

Case study · Manta Cares

The Problem

The strategic case for mobile was easy: notifications, widgets, a presence in her pocket, an app she opens like she opens everything else. The design case was harder, because the bridge from web to app is exactly where you lose people — and we were asking the least tech-confident audience imaginable to cross it.

Think about what we were actually requesting of Carol, our design persona from the dashboard case study: she's on our website, on a laptop her daughter set up, and we need her to end up with our app installed on her phone and logged in. Every standard pattern for this quietly assumes a comfort she doesn't have. "Scan this QR code" assumes she knows what that means and can aim a camera at her own screen. "You'll be redirected" assumes she won't panic when the screen changes. The transition had to be designed for someone who finds each of those steps genuinely uncertain.

Principle 1

Design the Bridge, Not Just the Destination

The handoff itself deserved real design attention, not a generic "Get the app" button. The centerpiece was a download modal, and the most important decision in it was a small one with a big rationale.

Standard practice is to make the QR code the hero. But for an older, less tech-confident user on a laptop, scanning a QR code is the hard path — it requires a second device, a camera app, and steady hands. Emailing herself a link she can simply tap on her phone is the easy path. So I designed the modal to lead with the email-link option and treat the QR code as the secondary route, inverting the usual hierarchy to match how our person actually behaves. Both routes point to a single, purpose-built app-download destination so the experience after the tap is consistent no matter how she got there.

Around the modal, we built app-promotion patterns into the web product — surfaces that invite the download at the right moments without nagging, tuned for an audience that needs encouragement, not pressure.

Principle 2

Don't Make the Returning User Start Over

A subtle but important failure mode: treating everyone who arrives logged-out as a brand-new visitor. For a returning patient, being shoved back through a sign-up funnel she already completed is confusing and a little insulting — I already did this.

So we put design effort into logged-out routing that distinguishes a returning user from a genuinely new one, and sends each to the right place — a returning user toward a familiar way back in, a new user toward a clean first-time setup. It's invisible when it works, which is the point. The same care showed up in the front door we'd already rebuilt: the login, sign-up, and reset-password pages on a real design system, and a simple, on-brand registration flow for the people who genuinely are new.

Principle 3

Ship, Watch, and Fix on the Real Device

You cannot design a mobile experience for this audience from a simulator. The only way to get it right was to put it in real hands and watch.

So we ran the rollout as a learning loop: internal TestFlight builds, then inviting real testers, then a steady stream of usability fixes driven by watching people stumble, and finally shipping into both the App Store and the Google Play Store so no one was excluded by their choice of phone. We've kept iterating since, right through recent map UX fixes on mobile. Underpinning all of it, we instrumented end-to-end product analytics — and gave real thought to how to measure newer surfaces like home-screen widgets — so the "watch" half of the loop was grounded in evidence, not vibes.

Conclusions

The web-to-mobile transition succeeded because we treated the bridge as a first-class design surface and refused to assume our user shared our comfort with technology.

  • We designed a download modal that leads with the easy path — a tappable emailed link — and demoted the QR code, with promotion patterns tuned for a gentle, non-nagging invitation.
  • We built logged-out routing so returning users never get treated like strangers.
  • We ran a ship-watch-fix loop on real devices and launched on both iOS and Android, backed by real analytics.
